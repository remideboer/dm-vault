Description  
  
Moander was an ancient deity of rot, corruption, and decay predating the empire of Netheril. Despite being banished from Toril on many occasions, the Jawed God always slithered back.  
  
Depicted variously as masculine, feminine, or simply “it.” Its most renowned avatar was the Abomination of Moander — a massive animated pile of rotting animal carrion and putrefied vegetation, covered in vines, lichen, moss, tendrils, eyes, and fanged maws. All mouths mumbled, screeched, and chanted Moander’s name in a demented chorus. The Abomination produced putrid black slime and absorbed any living or dead matter to grow larger.  
  
Manifestations  
  
Appeared to worshipers as a giant rotting male humanoid head with red eyes and 20-foot tendrils instead of hair. This form allowed communication, object manipulation, and creation of a mental link — which persisted after contact, whispering in the victim’s mind until removed by remove curse or remove disease.  
  
As god of decay, Moander could also manifest through any rot, sprouting 20-foot tentacles of decaying matter that could slam enemies, implant the seed of Moander into immobilized creatures, and feed on decay to spawn new tendrils.  
  
Powers  
  
In Abomination form, Moander could not cast spells, but compensated with sheer resilience. It could move across vertical surfaces, float on water, sprout tendrils and maws across its entire mass, and absorb most attacks. It required continuous organic matter to survive — without it, the Abomination rotted away within one to four months.  
  
Main attacks: tendril slams and a massive devouring maw. Also hurled tree trunks as projectiles and entangled enemies. Could implant the seed of Moander into captured creatures, taking full control. Those swallowed were crushed and rotted, their corpses added to its body.  
  
Vulnerability: fire. Large sources (such as dragon breath) could detonate the Abomination entirely. Cold also damaged and slowed it.  
  
Moander’s core could move between large piles of filth via a flying egg-shaped pod containing its essence. Could vomit a shambling mound twice per day and a gibbering mouther once every three days. These spawns were under its control for 1–12 days before gaining autonomy.  
  
Personality  
  
Cruel, controlling, tyrannical. Delighted in forcing mortals to ruin those they loved. Used lies and half-truths to cause emotional distress. Sought to corrupt anyone who refused to bow.  
  
Divine Realm  
  
Offalmound, in Rarandreth — the 223rd layer of the Abyss.  
  
Relationships  
  
Enemies: Lolth, Corellon Larethian, Hanali Celanil, Rillifane Rallathil, Solonor Thelandira, Arvoreen, Sheela Peryroyl, Jannath (aspect of Chauntea), Jergal, Mystryl, Selûne, Targus, Tyche, Bane (ancient rivalry), Phalse, Tyranthraxus.  
  
Allies: Kozah (aspect of Talos) — together sought to destroy Mystryl’s creations. Shar allied with both against Selûne.  
  
Moander especially relished corrupting beholders, possibly tied to ancient rivalry with Bane or enmity with the beholder-like fiend Phalse.  
  
Worshipers  
  
Moanderite worship preyed on lonely, adrift individuals who welcomed its firm direction. Never flush with worshipers, but numerous cults existed throughout history.  
  
Initiates underwent a ceremony where a seed of Moander was planted inside them, slowly turning their insides into rotting plant matter. Clergy were identifiable by a small flowering tendril protruding from the ear. The seed allowed Moander to directly assume control of any infected creature.  
  
Holy Day: Balefire — 1st of Hammer. Huge bonfires built to hold back the cold, one of the very few celebrations.  
  
Daily obligation: Ritual kill of a creature, or gathering vegetation to spread rot.  
  
Non-Human Worshipers  
  
Algoids, dark trees, gibbering mouthers, shambling mounds, vegepygmies, giant toads, giant slugs, black and green dragons. Dead enslaved beholders transformed into rotting death tyrants. In the late 14th century DR, some wild elves fearful of encroaching civilization also worshiped Moander.  
  
Places of Worship  
  
Typically sprawling underground complexes or hilltop locations in secluded wilderness, marked by circles of fang-shaped rocks around an altar. Underground temples built in places of rot, waste, and decaying sewage.  
  
Notable sites:  
  
	•	Abyss of the Abomination — Yûlash, Moonsea region. Over 1,000 years old by the 14th century DR. Oldest known Moanderite temple.  
	•	Hill of Fangs — outside Westgate. Eight red stone plinths shaped like curved inward fangs.  
	•	House of Moander — rumored shrine in forests outside Ylraphon.  
	•	Temple in Bezantur — Thay, City of a Thousand Temples.  
	•	Hidden Glades — near Torsch in Chondalwood. Active post-death temple led by Mouth of Moander Dalchatha Maereegh.  
  
History  
  
Ancient Netheril: Moander came to Toril and established his greatest temple in the Moander’s Footsteps mountains, annihilating an elder race by poisoning their food and water.  
  
Dawn Cataclysm: Moander corrupted the goddess Tyche through a rose that was actually a seed, causing her to rot from within. Selûne split Tyche to save her, birthing Tymora and Beshaba.  
  
75 DR: Moander’s “creeping evil” armies attacked the elven settlement of Tsornyl. Elven High Magic severed and imprisoned this aspect within the ruins, later renamed Darkwatch. Cost 32 elven lives including two High Mages of Myth Drannor.  
  
171 DR: The Coronal of Myth Drannor and sixteen High Mages bound the Abomination beneath the ruins of Yûlash, to be released only by an “unborn child.”  
  
1357 DR: Cultists allied with sorceress Cassana, lich Zrie Prakis, and the Fire Knives to create a living construct — the “nonborn child” — to free the Abomination. The construct gained sentience and became the hero Alias. Via the compulsory magic of azure bonds, Alias was lured to Yûlash and inadvertently freed Moander. The Abomination carved a path through Cormanthor toward Myth Drannor (later called Moander’s Road) before being destroyed over Westgate by the red dragon Mistinarperadnacles Hai Draco. Moander reduced to demipower status.  
  
1358 DR (Time of Troubles): Moander tracked down Dragonbait’s saurial tribe in Tarkhaldale, implanting his seed into Dragonbait’s mate Coral and using enslaved saurials to grow a new Abomination. Confronted by Alias, Dragonbait, and Finder Wyvernspur. Dragonbait killed Coral. Finder Wyvernspur and Akabar Bel Akash followed Moander into the Abyss and destroyed him with a shard of para-elemental ice. Finder inherited Moander’s divinity and became a demigod, sponsored by Tymora and Selûne.  
  
Post-death: Moander was not truly destroyed. His consciousness survived in Darkwatch, draining power from visitors via spores — either consuming them or influencing them to send more victims. Lolth seized the opportunity to claim Moander’s name as her surface-world aspect, attracting humans, elves, and half-elves drawn to rot and corruption.  
  
1374 DR: Strange fog appeared in the High Forest, turning living beings into rotting mummies bearing Moander’s holy symbol. Moanderite cultists attempted to use ancient Netherese magic to steal Finder Wyvernspur’s divine spark — unsuccessfully.  
  
15th century DR (Second Sundering): Moander’s influence returned to the Prime Material. The deity lay low, gathering power in patches of rot and corrupt minds. Offered warlock pacts in exchange for spreading rot and fear. Fueled his return by devouring several gods returned during the Second Sundering, including the demigoddess Kiputytto. Eventually deceived by Chosen of opposing gods into breaking his alliance with Shar.  
  
Rumors & Legends  
  
	•	A strange flying hand resembling Moander’s holy symbol haunted Brindul Alley in Waterdeep. Origin unknown.  
	•	The Citadel of Fungi near Moander’s Road was thick with yellow-green mold in the 15th century DR. Locals believed Moander’s influence still spread from the buried remains of the Abomination beneath a nearby hill.
